# Foundation Models and Generative AI

## Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT have revolutionized AI, enabling tasks from writing poetry to planning vacations.

These advancements highlight AI's potential to drive enterprise value.

## Foundation Models

Definition: A class of AI models trained on massive amounts of unstructured data in an unsupervised manner.

Origin: Term coined by Stanford researchers predicting a shift from task-specific models to general-purpose foundation models.

Key Feature: Ability to transfer across multiple tasks and domains.

## Generative AI Connection

Foundation models belong to Generative AI because they generate new content (e.g., predicting the next word in a sentence).

Core training task: Predicting and generating the next word based on previous words.

## Capabilities

Tuning: Adding small labeled datasets to adapt models for specific NLP tasks (e.g., classification, named entity recognition).

Prompting (Prompt Engineering): Applying models to tasks without additional training by crafting effective prompts.

Example: Sentiment analysis via prompt: 'Is this sentence positive or negative?'.

## Advantages

Performance: Trained on terabytes of data, outperforming models trained on limited datasets.

Productivity Gains: Requires less labeled data for task-specific adaptation due to pre-training.

## Disadvantages

Compute Costs: Expensive to train and run inference (requires multiple GPUs).

Trustworthiness: Data scraped from the internet may contain bias, hate speech, or toxic content.

Lack of transparency in training datasets.

## IBM Innovations

Improving efficiency, trustworthiness, and reliability of foundation models.

Applications across domains:

- \- Language: Watson Assistant, Watson Discovery

- \- Vision: Maximo Visual Inspection

- \- Code: Project Wisdom with Red Hat

- \- Chemistry: Moleformer for molecule discovery

- \- Climate: Earth science models using geospatial data

## Other Domains

Vision models (e.g., DALL-E2 for text-to-image generation).

Code models (e.g., GitHub Copilot).

Emerging areas: Chemistry, climate research.

## Key Takeaways

Foundation models are versatile, generative, and transformative.

They offer significant advantages but require addressing compute and trust issues.

IBM is actively innovating to make these models more efficient and reliable for business use.
